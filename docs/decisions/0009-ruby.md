# 0009 Ruby: every 振り仮名, from base to reading

Date: 2026-10-24
Status: proposed

## The problem

A 振り仮名 states how a run of characters was read at one place in one book. Some states the usual
reading (其（その）). Some gives a word the characters do not usually spell: 救荒事冝 (みんなで翻刻
`D69D7ED5F5A358D92D3B3BD0ED5C8BB6`, page 30, line 21) writes 村落（ざいご）, where ざいご is the
word usually written 在郷, and on the same line 支給（わりわた）and 逐月（まいげつ）.

The atlas reads these in two narrow ways. `atlas ruby-spellings` counts a fixed list of 32 readings
for the words layer (0004), and the `rubies` table (#685) links each ruby of a dataset's lines to
the units of its base. Neither keeps every pair, counts it over the corpus or shows it on the
site, so the crop of 村落 does not say that it was glossed ざいご.

## What the corpora hold

Ruby markup comes from the みんなで翻刻 transcriptions alone. `ar:`, `hl:` and `ex:` crops get their
text from the same transcriptions, so each is a view of that one source. Measured on the clone at
`be63dc2` (2026-09-06), parsed by `rubies.marks`:

| Entries with a ruby | 2,630 |
| Ruby fields | 1,481,022 |
| right (振り仮名), base holds Han | 1,451,438 |

| Base of one character / two / three or more / empty | 834,702 / 512,737 / 131,774 / 1,809 |

The largest projects are gakugei (283,567), iryotoyojo (258,422), galica (228,187) and honkokuv1
(171,191).

Other sources hold none. The NDL古典籍OCR学習用データセット has 103 lines with a bracket, most of
them unclosed (`附属（ふぞく`). CODH, 古活字, HNG, KHS, NLK, HI Lab, NDL OCR and Wikisource (zh) carry
no ruby. A ruby line drawn on a scan (`lines` role `ruby`) is the only other way one could enter,
and none is read yet.

How many rubies reach a published crop was last measured in #685 (17 October): 9,364 of 9,368 in
honkoku-atlas and 429 of 699 in ainu-characters, but 6,446 of 869,438 in honkoku-lines, whose lines
were mostly unaligned. Since then many `hl:` crops have been published (20x00015, the 落 of 村落, is
one). The current figure needs the mirror in the Codespace copy, so it is not measured here.

## The decision

### A pair links an rb n-gram to an rt n-gram

- **Pair:** `(rb, side, rt)`. `rb` is the base as `rubies.marks` delimits it, `rt` its reading with
  spaces removed and katakana mapped to hiragana, historical kana kept (`ruby_spellings` rule).
  `side` is `right` or `left`; a 左訓 is often a gloss or another language, so it is its own pair.
  A pair carries its occurrences, documents, projects, the forms as typed (`ゐなか` / `ヰナカ`), and
  the first and last dated documents.
- **Occurrence:** one per ruby field, with an id of its own built from the entry, page, line, the
  base offsets in the line's raw text and the side, so it holds across rebuilds while the
  transcription line is unchanged. It records the pair, book, page, line, offsets, the markup it came
  from, its tier, and the crops of its base in reading order when the line is aligned.
- **Base crops:** `rubies.link` gives dataset unit ids. `ruby.py` translates them through
  `out/mirror.sqlite` into the site's integer crop ids, as `reference.py` does for samples. A unit the
  mirror lacks, or whose crop is retired, leaves the list, so an occurrence names only crops the site
  can load; its line and offsets stay either way.
- **Tier:** `transcribed` for every occurrence today. The transcription does not say whether a ruby
  stands on the page or the transcriber supplied it (0004), so no occurrence is `observed` until a
  reviewer checks it on the scan (below).
- **Span:** a run keeps one reading (`group`). It is split into per-character readings only where
  the markup or a reviewer says so; the n-gram to n-gram pair is the unit.

### Kind is computed and labelled as such

Each pair gets a `kind` from `data/vocab/kanji-readings.json`: `per-character` when rt splits into
readings of rb's characters (allowing 連濁 and historical kana), or `whole` when it does not.
`whole` covers 熟字訓, 義訓 and transcriber errors alike, so it is a hint for search and never a
claim. 村落（ざいご） is `whole`; 村落（そんらく） is `per-character`.

### Links to words go through the words layer

A pair is never linked to another pair. 村落→ざいご reaches 在郷 through a word row, as 0004 links
spellings:

- `ja/ざいご/名詞` gets a row in `words.tsv`, with 在郷 as a spelling cited from
  Wiktionary 日本語版 「在郷」 revision 2017465, which gives the reading ざいご and the sense 田舎
  (under ざいごう).
- The pair yields an observed spelling 村落 of that word, source `atlas-ruby`, `word_by` `reading`:
  a ruby gives a reading, not a word, so the row says the word was matched by its reading.
- It joins the word only when rt equals the word's reading exactly. ざいご and ざいごう stay apart,
  as はかり and ばかり do in 0004. 在郷 is ruby'd ざいごう 9 times in 7 documents and never ざいご, so
  the corpus alone would not make the link; the dictionary row does.
- The card then shows 村落 under ざいご next to 在郷, with the 1 occurrence the ruby gives and the
  citation of each. Words get rows by hand, one cited entry at a time, so most of the 317,970 pairs
  link to no word and stand on their own pages.

This replaces `ruby_spellings.py` and `ruby-spellings.tsv`: the words layer's ruby source becomes a
filter over the pairs, so there is one parser and one count. A ruby occurrence on a crop is the
evidence for a `writes_word` claim with method `ruby`, and the inspector offers it when a reviewer
sets a word.

### Storage: built locally, served from R2, no new D1

The pairs change only when the clone is pulled again, and nothing a visitor does writes them. 0005
puts such data in R2.

- `atlas ruby build` writes `ruby.sqlite`: pairs, occurrences and a character index. It is the
  ruby system's own database, kept in `ATLAS3_DIR` and published as a download.
- `scripts/atlas3/ruby.py` writes R2 objects under `v3/ruby/` and uploads only those whose hash
  changed, as `reference.py` does:
  - one object per page with that page's rubies and their base crops, read by the crop and page
    views;
  - pairs sharded by each character of rb and by the first two kana of rt, for search;
  - one object per pair with its occurrences, for the pair page;
  - ranked lists (most attested, most `whole`, most readings per base) for browsing.
- **Rebuilds:** the pairs change only when the clone is pulled. The base crops change whenever a page
  is published, aligned or re-collected (0005 can retire a slot or allocate one). So `ruby.py` runs
  after every `publish.py` run, recomputes the page objects of the pages it touched, and rewrites the
  pair objects those pages feed.
- Measured size: 317,970 pairs averaging 20 bytes of rb and rt, 714,146 (character, pair) entries
  and 1.48M occurrences, about 60 MB gzipped. A reload writes only changed objects.
- **D1 writes: none for the pairs.** An own D1 database, as runs has, was costed: 317,970 pair rows plus indexes
  on rb and rt, and a 714,146-row character index, about 1.7M rows written once and 60 MB. Every
  answer above is a lookup by key that a sharded object serves, so D1 earns nothing for that cost.
  A query that needs a join across pairs would revisit this.
- **Words layer rows do reach D1.** The character card and search read `word_spellings` in D1, so
  the spellings a pair gives a listed word are loaded there by `reference.py`, as today. The filter
  must give the same rows `ruby-spellings.tsv` gives the words layer now, so the switch writes no
  row; each new word adds one row per spelling, one for ざいご (村落) today.
- **Checking a ruby:** a reviewer's check that a ruby stands on the page is a `bears_ruby` ledger claim whose
  subject is the occurrence id (`rb:<id>`), value `{side, rt}`, with one crop evidence per base crop
  at its current version. It holds only while every one of those crops keeps that version, so
  re-cutting any crop of the base reopens it; today the ledger checks only the subject crop's cut, and
  this needs that check for every crop the evidence names. Nested rubies have distinct ids, so two
  readings over one first crop stay apart. A few rows per check.

### Surfaces

- **Crop inspector:** a Ruby row lists the readings over this crop (`mono`) and over its word
  (`group`, the base shown with this crop marked), each linking to its pair, with a Check action
  for the ledger claim.
- **Page view:** the line transcription renders each ruby as `<ruby>` over its base; hovering a
  base outlines its crops on the scan.
- **Ruby browser**, `/ruby`:
  - search by rb, by any character in it, or by rt prefix (kana or rōmaji folded to kana);
  - a base page (村落: そんらく 29, むら〳〵 5, むらかた 2, ざいご 1, いなか 1, かたゐなか 1, …);
  - a reading page (ざいご: 村落, and through the word, 在郷);
  - a pair page with its occurrences as a crop gallery where aligned, otherwise line snippets with a
    link to the みんなで翻刻 page, with their documents and dates;
  - ranked lists of the most common pairs and of `whole` readings.
- **Character page:** a Ruby line beside Words, with the readings its crops carry, linking to the
  base page.

## Order of work

1. `atlas ruby build` and `ruby.sqlite`, with the table above as its test figures.
2. `scripts/atlas3/ruby.py`: R2 objects, dry run first.
3. Worker routes and the crop and page surfaces.
4. The `/ruby` browser.
5. The words-layer switch, replacing `ruby_spellings.py`, with the ざいご row.
6. The `bears_ruby` check, with evidence validity over every crop of the base.

## Consequences

- Every ruby the transcriptions hold becomes searchable and citable, with its book and line.
- Ruby stays `transcribed` until checked, so the site says what transcribers typed, not what the ink
  shows.
- No D1 rows for the pairs; the cost is R2 storage and an upload of changed objects per reload and
  per publication. The words layer keeps its rows in D1, one per spelling of a listed word.
- A ruby on an unaligned line reaches its page and its pair page, and reaches a crop only after
  alignment.
